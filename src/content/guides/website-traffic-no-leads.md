## Test the customer journey first

- Open the page on a phone and identify the offer without scrolling extensively.
- Follow the main CTA and complete validation using test data in a test environment.
- Check error and success states, not only the button appearance.
- Confirm that the destination receives an accepted inquiry.
- Check keyboard access, text size and whether overlays cover the form.

## Match the diagnosis to the evidence

| Observation | Possible next investigation |
|---|---|
| Relevant visits, no contact clicks | Offer, fit, proof and CTA clarity. |
| Contact clicks, few accepted submissions | Form friction, validation errors or delivery failures. |
| Many submissions, few qualified leads | Audience, promise, qualification and spam handling. |
| Qualified leads, few customers | Follow-up, pricing fit and the sales process. |

## Do not overinterpret small samples

A change from one inquiry to two is not strong evidence of a repeatable improvement. On low-traffic pages, use customer interviews and observed usability problems to prioritize fixes. Plan a statistical experiment only when the traffic and effect size support it.

## A useful improvement brief

Document the page, customer problem, evidence, proposed change and intended outcome. Keep a record of other changes that could affect the result. This makes the next review more useful than a collection of isolated design opinions.
