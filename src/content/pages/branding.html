Positioning before decoration

What the engagement needs to solve

Won Ads connects positioning, messaging and visual identity so customers can recognise the business and understand why it fits. Logo, colour and type decisions follow the strategy rather than replacing it.

Who this is for

New businesses, repositioning teams and growing brands with inconsistent communication across touchpoints.

Problems we address

  • A logo without a clear market position
  • Different messages across sales and marketing
  • Visual assets that cannot scale across formats
  • No rules for internal or partner use

What the scope can include

  • Discovery and positioning direction
  • Messaging or identity scope
  • Practical brand applications
  • Guidelines and handover

How performance is reviewed

Clarity, consistency, team adoption and the ability to produce future material efficiently; campaign response is measured separately.

Working process

We diagnose the current position, agree the scope and responsibilities, implement in reviewable stages, validate the result and use evidence to decide the next improvement.

Before work begins

Final deliverables, access, exclusions, timelines, revision or optimisation cadence and third-party costs are confirmed in the proposal. Unsupported claims are not added.

Questions before you start

Is branding only a logo?

No. A logo is one visual asset. Branding can also include positioning, messaging, colour, typography, applications and usage rules.

What do you need from the business?

Decision-maker access, audience context, offer details, competitors, current assets and honest feedback are useful inputs.

How long does a brand identity take?

Timing depends on discovery depth, number of applications and approval rounds. Milestones are confirmed after scope.

Are brand guidelines included?

Guidelines can be included at different levels, from a concise usage sheet to a broader identity system.

Can you update an existing identity?

Yes. We first identify what is useful, what is inconsistent and whether a refresh or larger repositioning is justified.

A practical starting point

Make your offer easier to recognize and understand

Branding connects positioning, messaging and visual identity. Start by clarifying who the business serves and why the offer matters before selecting logos, colours and layouts.

Positioning

Define the customer, problem, alternatives and credible reasons to choose the business. Avoid broad superlatives that could describe any competitor.

Identity in use

Test the identity on actual website sections, ads and sales materials. A logo presented in isolation does not demonstrate a usable system.

Handover

Agree source files, usage rights, guidelines and revision scope. Keep the website and marketing materials consistent after launch.

Questions to settle before commissioning the work

  • Who is the core audience?
  • What currently confuses customers?
  • Which assets and rights are required?
